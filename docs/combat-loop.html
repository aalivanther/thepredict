Combat systems

How a fight runs

Encounters, intents, the fallback chain, decision cadence and the per-tick layer that reacts between two decisions.

#Encounters

Bound mobs that fight the same targets form one encounter. An encounter sends at most one request per heartbeat (decision.encounterHeartbeatTicks, 20 ticks), with one question per mob, or sooner when something relevant happens: a contingency fires, the situation changes, or a skill runs predictordecide. A boss with three adds costs one request per heartbeat, not four.

  • A mob never joins an encounter that lists it as a participant: two ThePredict mobs fighting each other form two encounters, each with its own point of view.
  • Two groups that start fighting apart and later meet stay two encounters, with two requests.
  • An encounter carries at most 16 participants per capture, players and entities together.

#The life of an intent

  1. Adopted. An answer is adopted when its confidence reaches Timing.MinimumConfidence (0.45 by default) and, to replace the current tactic, beats it by Timing.SwitchMargin (0.15). After decision.lowConfidenceStreakForLocal low-confidence answers in a row the mob drops to its local planner (LOW_CONFIDENCE).
  2. Held. An adopted tactic is kept at least profileDefaults.minimumDecisionIntervalTicks (20 ticks).
  3. Renewed or expired. An intent is valid for profileDefaults.maximumIntentDurationTicks (40 ticks) unless renewed. Without a replacement it expires (EXPIRED).

Each request can also carry decision.contingenciesPerMob "what if" questions per mob (for example "if your health becomes critical"). If that situation happens, the mob switches to the stored answer without waiting for a new request.

An answer that arrives too late (decision.maxDecisionAgeMillis), after the mob changed target, after a reload, or from another model is discarded; /thepredict status counts each reason under discards.

Target changes. When MythicMobs changes the mob's target, the decision in flight is dropped and the mob plays at level 4 until the next capture of its encounter: 2 to 4 ticks usually, about 22 at most.

Retreats are shared. An encounter allows max(1, members / 3) simultaneous retreats; another mob asked to retreat falls back with RETREAT_LIMIT.

#Cadence

Timing.Cadence sets how often the mob asks:

CadenceIntent lengthCost
reactive (default)Short (Timing.IntentHorizon, 10 ticks), extended while nothing changes; reactive questions at most every Timing.ReactiveInterval (6 ticks).More questions. With the shipped request caps, the worst case is about one such encounter per world and up to about 0.95 USD per mob-hour.
standardThe usual intent length. IntentHorizon is ignored with a warning.The saving mode: about five encounters per world fit the same caps.

These are ceilings set by the request caps, not server measurements. See Jev connection for the budget keys.

#The per-tick layer

Between two decisions, the per-tick layer carries out the intent Jev chose. It never changes the tactic, the level or the deadline, except to extend a reactive intent (same tactic, longer deadline, no new question).

ModeWhat it adds
castsFires skills the moment they are ready.
reactions (default)Also reacts within ticks to openings and danger (table below), and times hits so they land.
fullAlso short micro-movements (circle, step aside, step in, back off).

The global mode is decision.tickLayer.mode; a profile's Timing.TickLayer may ask for less, never more (it warns and uses the global one, and so does an overlay). The layer has its own budgets under decision.tickLayer.*, and casting is never delayed when it degrades under load.

#Reactions

A reaction arms a preference for a few ticks: the next skill fired is one that suits the moment. Timing.Reactions picks the bands that are evaluated (all, survival or none), Reactions.Ahead the bands tried before the tactic's own skill (survival and opportunity by default), and Timing.ReactionDelay how long a trigger must hold first.

#Micro-movements

Only with full. The Paper goal thepredict:micro (priority 3) circles, steps aside, steps in or steps back toward short destinations computed with the pathfinder. It hovers around players only, and never asks for a path against air or after a hit it just took. Paper evaluates goals every 2 ticks, so a reaction can reach the legs 0 to 2 ticks later.

Impulses are yours. Leaps, dashes and knockbacks are your skills (leap, velocity and the other MythicMobs mechanics). ThePredict never calls setVelocity or applies knockback itself.
